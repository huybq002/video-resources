# Mystery task craft (the classic fair-play structure, our own cases)

Used by the night job for every `mystery` task (from 2026-11-15). Kept in data/ on purpose: it is a research product, not part of
the render look, so editing it never changes the approval fingerprint.

## Structure of one film, about 6-8 minutes
1. **The crime and the impossible situation** (about 1.5 min): a scene the viewer can SEE. Line 1 states the impossibility.
2. **Three suspects, three clues** (about 3 min): each suspect has an alibi that seems solid, shown in a picture. Three clues
   are drawn large and named once by the narrator. The narrator invites the viewer to guess.
3. **The reasoning** (about 2 min): the young detective takes the clues one by one; each cancels an alibi or a suspect; he names
   the killer, then SHOWS the trick step by step with a re-enactment drawing.
4. **Motive and the question** (30 s): the motive in two lines; a question the viewer can answer from their own life.

## The cast
A detective duo (a quiet sharp child and an older relative who voices the viewer's doubts), a police inspector, three suspects.
New names every film. Never a name, look or catch-phrase from an existing franchise.

## What the research step feeds in
- **Trick kind**: data/mystery-tricks.md, an `unused` kind. This is what the genre gives us.
- **Demand**: the digest (`data/research/latest.md`) and a WebSearch for what viewers search about locked-room mysteries, alibi
  puzzles and "how the trick worked" videos: titles, lengths, which kinds of trick draw views. Use the words people type
  (never a franchise's name) in the search phrase and tags.
- **Freshness**: `data/ideas.md` rows with `mystery`; never the same kind or the same victim type twice in a week.

## Packaging
Title: the impossibility in a promise ("The Study Locked From the Inside"), 40-60 characters. Thumbnail text: three words naming the
puzzle ("LOCKED FROM INSIDE", "9:07 PM"). Description first line: the impossible situation, then "can you spot the three clues".
Labels last (fiction, AI), as growth-playbook.md says.
