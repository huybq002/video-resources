# Story formats and script craft (from the reference channel)

Studied 2026-11-08: IfverseTales (19 long videos, 14 Shorts; top: "What If Your Reaction Time Was 10x Faster?" 4.6M,
"What If You Had 500 IQ?" 1.4M, "What If One Second Felt Like Ten?" 691k) - transcripts of "Reaction Time",
"I Have 100% Luck", "One Second Felt Like Forever", "Everyone Has a Power Level", plus frame studies. We take the
craft, not their stories or characters: every idea, name and line we write is our own.

## 1. Pick a format first (`"format"` in spec.json)

| format | Voice | Shape | Cast and places | Their example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `what_if` | 2nd person, present ("You catch it.") | demo -> question/title -> rule -> test -> escalation ladder -> cost -> choice / cliffhanger | "you" (hero in one signature outfit), 1 friend, 1 mentor or rival, everyday people; a NEW everyday place for each ladder step | Reaction Time, Power Level, 500 IQ |
| `character` | 3rd person, past/present, a named hero ("This is Jonah Pike.") | intro + promise of danger -> montage of wins, each in a new place -> the price -> the person who pays it -> turn | hero, a planted stranger ("the man in the yellow coat - remember him"), an antagonist, a love/friend interest | I Have 100% Luck |
| `episode` | 2nd person, continues a series | cold open on the climax ("The rain has stopped...") -> "Your name is Max" recap -> new rule -> new character -> emotional cost -> cliffhanger | recurring cast (hero, mentor, the first of their kind, a friend who lost himself); one lab/base + the big set piece | One Second Felt Like Forever (FULL MOVIE parts) |
| `days` | 2nd person, a counter | "Day 1: 6 MPH." each step doubles, a HUD counter, each day a bigger place | hero + the people who notice | Day 1: 6 MPH. Day 28: Faster Than Light |
| `dilemma` (Shorts) | 2nd person, 40-60 s | situation -> the two choices -> stakes -> question to the viewer | 2-3 people, one place | There's Room for One. You or Him? / The Ball... or the Boy? |
| `explainer` | narrator, 2nd person | everyday hook -> the mechanism in 3 steps -> the surprising number -> what it means for you | one person + diagrams/screens | (our psychology/money topics) |
| `wordless` (Shorts) | no narration, only reactions/SFX | one action told in 8-12 shots: prepare -> do -> surprise -> payoff | 1 person + 1 animal/object | caveman cooking Short |

Our channel topics map: money stories -> `character` or `what_if`; behavioural psychology -> `explainer` or `what_if`;
invented mysteries -> `character` or `episode`. Shorts -> `dilemma`, `what_if` (40 s) or `wordless`.

## 2. Line craft (measured)

- **Short sentences**: 5-7 words per sentence on average (theirs: 5.3, 5.6, 6.5, 8.4). One-word payoffs: "Caught." "Shot." "Fear."
- **Show before you tell**: open on the impossible moment, in concrete physical detail, before any explanation.
  "A fly buzzes past your face. You don't even think. You just catch it."
- **The title question lands at 20-40 s**, after 2-3 demonstrations, said once, with a `title` card on screen.
- **One rule + one limit = the whole plot engine**, said in plain words and repeated later as a motto:
  "Your brain got ten times faster. Your muscles did not. You can't move faster - you just start earlier."
- **Make it measurable**: a test with a normal number vs yours ("A normal person: thirty centimetres. You? Three millimetres.")
  and a scale ("average adult 10, a cop 120, the heavyweight champion 850"). Put every number on screen (`counter`, `chip`, `tag`).
- **Explain causes as a chain of three**: "Your eyes see, your brain decides, your muscles move." -> `chain` HUD.
- **Escalation ladder**: everyday -> social -> dangerous -> public -> life-changing. Each step is 2-4 lines: setup / beat /
  one-word payoff, in its own place. Transition lines: "Here's how it works." "But where it gets really crazy is..."
- **Specific, funny details** make it feel true: "He only bought it because they were out of gum." "a coin with a frog on it."
  "Your neighbour who never shuts up about his bench press: 14."
- **Contrast pairs**: "Normal players guess. You don't." Someone next to the hero always loses what the hero wins.
- **Plant and pay off**: point at something early and tell the viewer to remember it ("The man in the yellow coat. Remember him.").
  Pay it off in the last third. Write both in the spec (`plant` / `payoff` on the lines).
- **The cost turns the story at 55-70 %**: "But you can't unsee it." "It's going to cost you everything." The fun part ends there.
- **Other characters speak little**, short and in reaction: "How did you do that?" "They think I'm cheating."
- **Leave room for action**: about 1.5-2.0 spoken words per second in their films; the gaps are action with sound.
  We keep 2.2-2.6 w/s (no music bed yet), so write fewer words and more `do` actions, not longer sentences.
- **End** on a choice, a cliffhanger line, or a question to the viewer; series episodes end mid-crisis.

## 3. Titles that work

- "What If You [had X]?" / "What If [everyday thing] Was 10x [more]?"
- "I Have 100% [Luck]... But It's Not Mine." (power + twist)
- "Everyone Has a [Power Level]. Mine Is 1." (world rule + underdog number)
- "Day 1: [small]. Day 28: [impossible]." (counter + extreme)
- Shorts: "[Situation]. You or Him?" / "He's 5. He's lived 64 lives."

## 4. Visual devices that go with the craft (spec-schema.md)

`tag` over every head for a world rule with numbers - `chain` for the 3-step cause - `counter` for the test result - `split` for
normal vs you - `speedlines` + `flash` when the power fires - `screen` for apps/tickets/balances - time-jump `chip`
("TWO DAYS EARLIER") - a night `shopfront` establishing shot for each new place - slow `push_slow` on the frozen moment.
