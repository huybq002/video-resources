# From idea + duration to a script

| Duration | Scenes (25–35 s) | Cues (≈3.8 s) | Words (≈2.4 × s) | Shots (≈6–9 s each) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 s | 1 | 8 | 70 | 4 |
| 60–70 s | 2 | 16–18 | 150–170 | 8–9 |
| 3 min | 6 | 47 | 430 | 24 |
| 10 min | 20 | 158 | 1440 | 80 |

**Shot budget for a film feel** (the table above is cues; shots are what the viewer feels): aim for a median shot of 3–5 s → **10 min ≈ 140–220 shots, 3 min ≈ 45–65, 1 min ≈ 15–22**. Never let more than ~3 consecutive shots use the same framing (wide/medium/close-up) or the same setting without a different angle. A 10-minute story needs ≥ 8 distinct settings (use `lib:` ones), 12–20 scenes, ≥ 40 % of shots as medium or close-ups of faces, 1–2 insert/cutaway shots per sequence, and one wide establishing shot each time the place changes. Write dialogue as `dialogue` sequences (≈ 6–10 lines each) and connective narration as `narration` sequences; hand-write only the key moments.

Long videos: keep a library of 6–10 reusable settings and ≤ 5 characters; reuse them with different cameras/blocking.

Structure (scale proportionally): **hook** (first 5–10 s, strongest image/line, open loop, no greeting) → setup (who/where/what) → core in 3 beats, a new fact/question/turn every 30–45 s → turn/twist with 3–5 planted clues → takeaway (1–2 sentences) → specific CTA (a question that makes viewers comment + subscribe). Do not reveal the full answer before the last third.

Cue style: spoken, rhythmic, ≤ 14 words, one idea; vary lengths; punctuation = pauses. Vietnamese: correct diacritics; short names; write numbers as words (TTS mis-reads numerals).

Safety: fiction by default; invented names; no real people/brands; imply danger off-screen; no instructions for harm; morally ambiguous characters still face consequences.

Visual rhythm: change something visible every 3–6 s (shot cut, camera push, pose/expression, prop drop, FX). Use `cut:true` + `punch` camera for revelations, `shake` for impacts, `flash` after the hook.
